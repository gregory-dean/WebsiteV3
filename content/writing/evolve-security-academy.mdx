## The short version

After an intense and transformative few months, I completed the Evolve Security Academy bootcamp and earned my ESCP certification. From day one the program was immersive and demanding, designed to mirror the pace and pressure of real-world security operations rather than slide decks. I got hands-on time with threat hunting, red teaming, malware analysis, and live incident response scenarios, and it reshaped how I think about security, persistence, and problem solving.

## The real engagement

The part I'm proudest of wasn't a lab. Through Evolve, I supported a team based penetration test of a live client environment for the Drug Policy Alliance, working across reconnaissance, validation, and reporting.

- Conducted internal and external assessments with Nmap, Nessus, Burp Suite, and Dirbuster
- Identified and documented 24 validated vulnerabilities with CVSS based prioritization
- Wrote findings that translated technical vulnerabilities into remediation steps both technical and non-technical stakeholders could act on
- Built a Python script on the HIBP API to automate the client's email exposure checks for the report

That last item eventually grew into my [Email Breach Checker](/writing/email-breach-checker) project.

## What changed in how I work

- **Think in paths, not tools.** Attackers chain misconfigurations, weak controls, and human factors. Mapping those paths, often with MITRE ATT&CK, matters more than memorizing product UIs.
- **Offense sharpens defense.** Running the exploit makes the detection gap obvious. That loop is now how I approach all my lab work.
- **Write for the team that has to live with the finding.** Clear remediation beats clever severity scores nobody can act on.

## The people

I'm grateful for the mentors, instructors, and teammates who pushed and supported me through the long nights of simulations and rapid-fire labs. Their guidance made the difference, and it set the bar for the kind of teammate I want to be.

## Where I'm headed

This was a major step, but just the beginning. I'm continuing to build through my homelab and public projects, and I'm looking for SOC Analyst and Junior Penetration Tester roles where I can keep growing under experienced practitioners while contributing real coverage.
